Welcome to the world of value investing!

You don't need to spend your days anxiously glued to the market tickers, and you don't have to lose sleep over it. Value investing is a much more calm and composed way to invest.

It’s about casually reading research over a cup of coffee, and only buying when the price is right.

This isn't just an investment strategy; it's practically a philosophy for life.

So, before we begin this journey, we're not going to jump straight into the techniques. Instead, we want to first share what's most important: the 'value investing worldview.

Using Financial Ratios

You don't have to read the financials cover to cover. Ratios are a shortcut to help you quickly grasp the key numbers.

Profit Margins

This number tells a story. It shows you if the company has 'pricing power' and helps you spot its 'core competitive edge' just by reading the P&L.

ROE & ROA

These measure efficiency and quality. They basically answer the question: Is management actually any good at putting your money to work?

Dividend Yield

Is higher always better? Not necessarily. A super-high yield can be a red flag. You need to understand the nature of that dividend first.

Free Cash Flow

Philosophy

When it comes to investing, keep it simple. What really lets you sleep soundly at night isn't a bunch of complex techniques—it's having the right 'investment philosophy'.

Investment Moat

If a great business is like a strong castle, what protects it and keeps it standing strong? It's a 'moat'—one that's wide, deep, and maybe even filled with crocodiles. We call this an 'Investment Moat'.

Margin of Safety

The investing world is full of uncertainty. We can't predict the next recession, shifts in the industry, or even what a company will report next quarter. That's why we need to leave ourselves a 'margin of safety'—by buying at a price far below its 'intrinsic value'—to give ourselves a cushion for any mistakes.

Patience

Most people live like anxious 'stock-price tenants' rather than calm-and-steady 'business owners.' Why does it seem to make perfect sense to calmly hold a farm for decades, but people can't apply that same mindset to the stocks they own?

Circle of competence

Value investing doesn't require you to be an expert in every field. Staying within the circle of competence you know best is more than enough to build wealth. When you run into something you don't understand, admitting it and walking away is the safest way to protect your capital.

Value Trap

You think you're buying a cheap stock, but you might actually be buying a business that is slowly dying.

Non-Operating Income

Non-operating income isn't just a random number; it can also be an integrated part of the business model.
